# Play declaration review — 15 October 2026

Review of repository behavior and official guidance; no Console form, release, upload or account setting was changed. The handoff reports saved answers **No data collected or shared** and **no financial features**. Treat both as **NEEDS REVIEW**, not evidence of compliance. This review does not certify the declarations.

## What the source actually does

- `MerchantLookup.kt` sends approved business names or one explicitly consented complete VPA to Brave over HTTPS, using the user's API key in `X-Subscription-Token`. Defaults are disabled; a second optional setting enables automatic recognized-brand queries. No SMS body, amount, date, bank/account field or note is deliberately added. VPA consent is per address, not automatic-brand consent.
- `modules/kanakku-native/android/build.gradle` bundles `com.google.mlkit:text-recognition:16.0.1`. Its merged manifest contains ML Kit initialization and Google data-transport components. Bundled OCR is not proof of zero telemetry.
- Installed `expo-updates` `FileDownloader.kt` sends `EAS-Client-ID` on update/asset requests and can send a previous startup failure in `Expo-Fatal-Error`. These are source observations, not a network-capture result. No separate Expo Observe integration was found in the inspected dependency/config files.
- SMS parsing, speech decoding and OCR inputs/results remain on device; confirmed journal/receipt fields persist in SQLCipher. The camera creates a temporary cache photo before normal cleanup. Local diagnostics are not automatically uploaded by Kanakku.

## Data safety: changes and decisions

Google distinguishes off-device collection from local access. SDK transmissions count. Local-only SMS, purchase records, audio and images are outside collection; user-directed sharing exceptions do not remove collection obligations. Optional collection must genuinely be avoidable by every user. [Official Data safety guidance](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469?hl=en).

**Top-level collection answer: Yes for the current SDK/network-enabled app.** Do not restore the earlier draft's SMS/financial collection checkboxes merely because those fields are stored locally. Use this mapping to review the shipped artifact and live form:

For ML Kit, Google documents HTTPS technical collection for diagnostics/usage analytics, including device/app information, installation identifiers, feature events and performance data. Recognition content itself stays on device. Its disclosure page describes latest SDKs: confirm the shipped version rather than assuming a privacy toggle exists. [ML Kit disclosure](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure), [privacy terms](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/terms).

For Expo, its documentation describes a random installation token and technical update metadata; local source confirms the client-ID header and possible startup-error header. Treat identifier collection as relevant even though it is not a hardware ID. [Expo privacy explanation](https://expo.dev/privacy-explained), [security overview](https://expo.dev/security).

**Ephemeral:** do not select Yes for ordinary Brave search logs. Its current notice says queries may be kept up to 90 days for billing/troubleshooting, with legal qualifications; enterprise zero retention is not established for this BYOK account. ML Kit analytics/update metadata also lack evidence of memory-only request processing. [Brave API privacy notice](https://api-dashboard.search.brave.com/documentation/resources/privacy-notice).

**Optional/required:** Brave collection is optional: journal use works with lookup disabled. Manual update requests can be avoided. For ML Kit and the union of installation-ID/diagnostic types, inspect initialization behavior first: optional camera permission alone does not prove that all technical collection is optional. The merged manifest initializes ML Kit independently of the camera screen. If users cannot avoid collection, the form must reflect that.

**Shared:** do not automatically mark every provider transfer shared, or blanket-mark No. Specific manual searches may qualify for the user-directed/prominent-consent exception; automatic brand searches need their own assessment of the actual disclosure/consent. A service-provider exception requires the provider relationship to qualify. Brave's notice excludes search-query data from its DPA, so do not assume its general DPA settles that question. ML Kit's vendor disclosure says it does not onward-share the listed data. Document the basis before choosing the per-type sharing answers.

**Security/deletion:** application endpoints use HTTPS; validate the released SDK/redirect behavior before the all-data encryption assertion. Reset/uninstall removes local data, not provider logs. Do not promise provider deletion through local Reset; inspect actual provider procedures before answering deletion-request questions. No Kanakku account exists. [Android category examples](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/declare-data-use) can help resolve the remaining form mapping.

Required above does **not** mean camera permission or optional lookup must be granted: it reflects that the present app has no verified all-user opt-out from included SDK technical collection. The manifest's ML Kit initializer establishes automatic initialization, not proof that a particular payload was sent at launch. If an actual audit demonstrates that all collection of a type is avoidable, revise its optional/required answer then.

**Why No sharing is supportable for technical SDK data:** Google's own Play-disclosure guidance says its listed ML Kit metrics are not transferred to third parties. Use that as the SDK provider's documented answer for those metrics, not as a general rule that Google receiving app data is never sharing. For Expo, [Terms §3.2](https://expo.dev/terms) explicitly describe processor handling in the Services; this supports Play's service-provider exception for operational update data. Its aggregate/de-identified improvement role is also disclosed there, so reassess if adding independent provider purposes or further SDKs. Neither exception removes collection.

**Why automatic Brave needs care:** per-payment VPA review is an explicit, user-initiated transfer: the screen identifies the complete address, names Brave, warns it may identify a person, and requires a separate affirmative switch. Automatic brand requests run from a persistent Settings opt-in. Google's [prominent disclosure requirements](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311?hl=en) require unexpected collection/sharing disclosures during normal use, without requiring navigation into Settings. The current Settings paragraph/switch therefore does not substantiate the prominent-disclosure exception for background transaction-derived searches. The CSV currently omits Purchase history even though automatic lookup sends the merchant label derived from a payment; review adding it as collected and shared (optional, App functionality). The current CSV also marks User payment info as shared, but the only such payload is a manually consented payee VPA; that sharing answer appears over-inclusive under Google's user-initiated-action exception. Review the BYOK auth token separately because it is sent in the request header. These are evidence-based mappings to verify with Play's definitions, not a Google ruling for this app.

## Financial features: literal options and recommendation

Google's published declaration taxonomy lists **Support services → Other**, alongside credit monitoring/reporting, financial advice and insurance. It does not list a dedicated “Personal finance / budgeting” option. “My app doesn't provide any financial features” is the alternative for an app without financial features. [Official declaration guide](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13849271?hl=en-GB).

Google's broader policy includes money-management products, so the absence of payments or lending alone does not establish that an expense journal has no financial features. [Financial services policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821?hl=en-GB).

Kanakku's actual feature is SMS-derived personal spending management with recurring-charge insights. **Current root handoff:** the live form has a separate Other category with a description, and root reports saving a factual SMS-expense-tracking explanation as a draft. This replaces the earlier “none” recommendation in the working draft; it has not been certified or finally submitted by this review. Other is the candidate fit for this scope, an interpretation rather than an explicit Google ruling about this app. If the form explanation remains unclear, ask Play support before final submission. Do not select banking, payments/transfers, loans, investments or financial advice merely because the app displays bank alerts and descriptive totals.

Suggested factual explanation if the form permits it:

> Personal expense tracking: supported bank transaction SMS are parsed locally into an encrypted spending journal with categories, recorded recurring-charge patterns and historical totals. The app does not open bank accounts, move money, lend, trade, insure or provide personalized financial advice.
